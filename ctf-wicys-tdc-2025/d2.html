D2. Not-so-Simple Mail Protocol

★☆☆☆☆ EASY 100 PTS INVESTIGATION WiCyS + Target Defense Challenge 2025: BURROWING THE BUSINESS

>_ Identify the first extortion email sent to the organization.

Challenge

We've received a menacing extortion email from an unknown sender, claiming that sensitive data has been stolen from the company. The email demands a ransom and threatens to release the data if the demand is not met within 48 hours.

Unfortunately, it's clear based on the extortion email that the sender attempted to send this email before... Since the demand's due date is relative, we need to find the first email sent so we can estimate a lower bound on the deadline for the legal and finance teams to plan around.

Can you trace the digital breadcrumbs and find the first extortion email sent by this threat actor?

Objective

Our trusty log dashboard Insightful Horizon is back! Use it to pivot and find the first extortion email from this incident.

Once you've found it, use the email address that sent the email as the flag.

Note

This challenge was created earlier in 2025, so the last data you'll see is from March.

Flag Format

An email address, for example:

example@wicys.example

This flag IS NOT case-sensitive.

Required Tools

  • Web Browser

Additional Resources

  • OpenSearch Dashboards docs
  • Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP)
  • Zeek SMTP docs

Solution

The Target team provided us the link to the log dashboard inside of the challenge. The dashboard link has been omitted from this write-up as to prevent unauthorized access. Usernames and passwords have also been redacted. The first step is to log in to the dashboard.

In the Search Query, we will first type in 2 words separated by an AND argument: urgent AND data

OpenSearch Dashboards — query "urgent AND data" returns the D1 email  |  Click to enlarge

We got a hit! This is the email we saw in the previous challenge D1. We confirmed our query worked. Now we have to find the very first email that was sent to our organization. The next step is to search for terms to help us find the very first email. In the next query, we are going to use the words have AND data. We are going to keep it simple as we pull other words from the first query.

OpenSearch Dashboards — query "have AND data" surfaces an earlier email  |  Click to enlarge

We got a second hit! This time we can see an email was sent to our organization one day prior. Let's expand the query.

Expanded log entry — mainfrom field reveals the sender email address  |  Click to enlarge

Searching through the metadata we can see an email address, mainfrom, of the sender. This value is our flag — comparing the timestamps of the two email samples, this was an earlier email sent to the organization. The subject line is also an indicator that the two emails must be related.

MITRE ATT&CK

In this challenge, we leveraged the Personalyz.io SIEM tool to query logs and identify the first extortion email sent from Shadow Gopher.

Tactics

In the MITRE ATT&CK framework, this tactic falls under TA0040 — Impact as the contents of the email imply that data has been exfiltrated and the attacker is extorting the organization for monetary gain.

Techniques

In the MITRE ATT&CK framework, this technique falls under T1657 — Financial Theft, as the adversary is threatening to expose the data publicly if the ransom demands are not paid on time.

Procedures

Shadow Gopher sent multiple emails to Personalyz.io users demanding a ransom payment within a time limit or else the adversary will publish sensitive company and PII data.

Mitigation

The following mitigation measures can be applied to this TTP. Click a link to view full details in the MITRE ATT&CK Framework.

MitigationDescription
M1018 — User Account Management Implement and enforce policies for the lifecycle of user accounts including enforcing the Principle of Least-Privilege, implementing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), strong password policies and account lockout policies, and properly deactivating dormant accounts when not in use.
M1017 — User Training Educate employees and contractors to recognize, report and prevent cyber threats that involve phishing, social engineering and other human manipulative techniques. Security awareness training, phishing simulations and role-based training are examples of user training.

It is important to note, all employees and contractors should have an easy-to-use method for reporting emails in their email client.
